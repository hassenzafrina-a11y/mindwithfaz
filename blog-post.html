You Don't Need to Be in Crisis to Start Therapy

There's a quiet belief a lot of people carry without saying it out loud: that therapy is for when things fall apart. That you need a reason big enough to justify taking up someone's time. In my experience, that belief keeps people waiting far longer than they need to.

Most of the people who sit across from me aren't in crisis. They're tired. They're stuck on something that won't resolve itself. They're going through a change — a relationship shifting, a job ending, a version of themselves they're trying to grow out of — and they want somewhere to think it through out loud, with someone who isn't going to just tell them it'll be fine.

Reaching out early is often the easier path, not the harder one.

Waiting for things to get bad enough doesn't make the work easier. It usually just means there's more to untangle by the time you start. Coming in earlier — while things are still manageable, while you still have the energy to reflect rather than just cope — tends to make the process gentler and faster.

So if you've been sitting with the thought of reaching out, turning it over, wondering if it's "enough" of a reason — it is. You don't need to be in crisis. You just need to be curious about doing things differently.
